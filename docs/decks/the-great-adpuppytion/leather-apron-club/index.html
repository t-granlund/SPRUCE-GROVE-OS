Act I · Philadelphia · 1727

A 21-year-old printer gathers twelve tradesmen —
leather aprons and all.

The Junto

Not a debate society. A civic problem-solving incubator.

Twelve members. Every Friday evening. Curiosity, observation, and trade experience translated into action — unmoored from religion, dogma, or class.

No grandstanding. Every inquiry had to end somewhere useful: a discovery shared, a law amended, a citizen helped.

"I had form'd most of my ingenious acquaintance into a club of mutual improvement, which we call'd the Junto." Benjamin Franklin · Autobiography

Admission — four inquiries, answered standing

01

"Have you any particular disrespect to any present members?"

02

"Do you love mankind in general, of what religion or profession soever?"

03

"Do you think any person ought to be harmed in his body, name, or goods, for mere speculative opinions, or his external way of worship?"

04

"Do you love truth for truth's sake — and will you endeavor impartially to find and receive it yourself, and communicate it to others?"

Three hundred years later these still screen out almost everyone. That's the point.

The operating code

No contradiction combat. No certainty.

Direct contradiction and dogmatic assertion were fined. Words like "certainly" and "undoubtedly" cost you coin.

Opinions arrived provisionally: "I conceive," "it appears to me at present." Pragmatism over metaphysics — the room existed to find what works, not who's right.

"No sect, no party, no class. Workers in leather aprons, thinking out loud without armor." The Junto method, in one sentence

Act II · Every era, same tremor

Every new medium
creates a panic class.

Before the art arrives, the fear arrives first. Ours is called "AI slop." Theirs had names too.

The pattern · four panics, one shape

1896

Film arrives. Gorky calls it a gray, lifeless shadow of theater.

1927

The Vitaphone. Theaters pay for sound by firing their orchestras — ~20,000 musicians gone within two years.

1942

Recording. Petrillo bans it nationwide — over two years. Ads show a robot feeding instruments into a meat grinder.

1969

The Moog. The musicians' union bans the synthesizer from union work. A decade later it's Stevie Wonder.

The fear was real — orchestras did collapse. The panic just never predicted what humans did next.

1927 → 1940 · The last time it felt this heavy

When Chaplin finally spoke, he didn't make small talk.

The greatest silent artist on earth refused the talkies for thirteen years. Europe under occupation, America still neutral — and then The Great Dictator ends with him addressing humanity directly.

What he said then is what this club is for now.
Here it is. All of it. His words, not ours.

The Great Dictator · 1940 · Verbatim · I of VIII

"I'm sorry, but I don't want to be an emperor. That's not my business. I don't want to rule or conquer anyone. I should like to help everyone if possible — Jew, Gentile, black man, white."

Verbatim · II of VIII

"We all want to help one another. Human beings are like that. But we have lost the way."

Verbatim · III of VIII

"Machinery that gives abundance has left us in want. Our knowledge has made us cynical. Our cleverness hard and unkind. We think too much and feel too little. More than machinery, we need humanity. More than cleverness, we need kindness and gentleness."

Verbatim · IV of VIII

"The airplane and the radio have brought us closer together. The very nature of these inventions cries out for the goodness in men."

Verbatim · V of VIII

"Even now my voice is reaching millions throughout the world — millions of despairing men, women, and little children. To those who can hear me, I say: do not despair. The hate of men will pass, and dictators die, and the power they took from the people will return to the people. And so long as men die, liberty will never perish."

Verbatim · VI of VIII

"Soldiers — don't give yourselves to brutes. Men who despise you, enslave you, who regiment your lives, tell you what to do, what to think, and what to feel. Who drill you, diet you, treat you like cattle, use you as cannon fodder. Don't give yourselves to these unnatural men — machine men with machine minds and machine hearts."

Verbatim · VII of VIII

"You are not machines. You are not cattle. You are men."

Verbatim · VIII of VIII

"You, the people, have the power — the power to create machines, the power to create happiness. You, the people, have the power to make this life free and beautiful, to make this life a wonderful adventure. Then, in the name of democracy, let us use that power — let us all unite."

Why we read all of it

"Machine men with machine minds" was the warning.

"The power to create machines" was the assignment.

Our generation gets both at once. Nobody gets through it alone.

What survives every wave

Risk. Scarcity. Connection.

Risk — put your name on something before it's safe. Scarcity — be someone, not everyone. Connection — show up in the room, again and again, until you're missed when you don't.

None of that can be generated. All of it compounds — the way a library, a fire brigade, and a university compounded out of a bar.

Act V · So we named it

THE LEATHER
APRON CLUB

Meetings: every Friday evening. Membership: twelve, tight.
Aprons: optional. Questions: mandatory.

The rules — adapted from 1727, verbatim where it matters

Composition · 8–12 max

Cross-functional practitioners — barbers, printers, welders, engineers, organizers. Not commentators.

Epistemic code

Provisional phrasing. Falsification over advocacy. Disagreement is about data, constraints, mechanism — never identity.

The rotating inquiries

Technical intelligence · civic gap analysis · mutual aid. "How can the Junto assist you in any of your honorable designs?"

Action subsidiarity

Every agenda item yields an experiment, a document, a prototype, or a civic pilot — inside 30 to 90 days. Talk is not output.

The pipeline · Franklin's, running in Bentonville this week

1 · Observe

A Friday inquiry names the friction. Example: a barbershop with no website — a trade built on walking traffic, invisible online.

→

2 · Inquire

Shared research, cross-discipline review. Mock-up built in the Spruce Grove design system, speaking the shop's own values.

→

3 · Mobilize

Made public: progress pages, demos, warm introductions. Receipts over reassurance — you can check the work.

→

4 · Institute

Self-sustaining prototype: a live site, a lead platform, a neighbor who has air conditioning. Outlasts the meeting.

Act VI · Three commitments · the only contract

01

Show up

Fridays are sacred. You can't be in a circle you don't attend. This hour belongs to the room.

02

Bring truth

One observation and one honorable design, phrased provisionally. Zero invented facts — everything checkable.

03

Lift one burden

Every meeting ends with a name and an ask we can actually answer — then 30–90 days to answer it.
